Three-Person Group Presentation

Supporting a Student Experiencing Bullying Based on Sexual Orientation

This presentation explains how an Education Assistant can respond, provide support, and adapt strategies according to age and developmental needs.

1

Presenter 1: Immediate Support and EA Responsibilities

Imagine a student approaches you and says they are being bullied because of their sexual orientation. As an Education Assistant, the first thing I would do is listen carefully and respectfully. I would thank the student for trusting me and reassure them that bullying is not acceptable.

My role is to help the student feel safe and supported. I would document what the student tells me and follow school procedures by informing the supervising teacher, counselor, or administrator. I would also help the student identify trusted adults and safe places in the school where they can go if they feel unsafe.

The most important message is that every student deserves respect and has the right to learn in a safe environment.

2

Presenter 2: Strategies for Support

There are several ways an EA can support a student experiencing bullying.

First, we can provide emotional support by listening without judgment and validating the student’s feelings. Second, we can work with teachers, counselors, and administrators to ensure that incidents are addressed appropriately.

We can also encourage inclusion by helping create a welcoming classroom environment where diversity is respected. If the school has a Gender and Sexuality Alliance, or GSA, students may benefit from connecting with peers who provide support and understanding.

Finally, we should encourage students to report bullying and remind them that seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness.

3

Presenter 3: Age and Disability Considerations

The basic approach to supporting a student remains the same regardless of age or ability: ensure safety, provide support, and follow school policies.

However, the way we provide support may change depending on the student’s age. Younger students may need simpler explanations and more direct guidance, while teenagers may want more privacy and independence when discussing their experiences.

Support is also not dependent on whether a student has a developmental disability. Every student deserves protection from bullying. However, some students may need additional supports, such as visual aids, social stories, or communication assistance, to help them understand the situation and express their feelings.

Conclusion

Education Assistants play an important role in creating safe and inclusive schools. While support strategies may be adapted to a student’s age, communication style, or developmental needs, every student deserves respect, acceptance, and protection from bullying.

Suggested speaking time: about 1–1.5 minutes per presenter.
