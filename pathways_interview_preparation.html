Pathways to Education

Volunteer Phone Interview Preparation

20-minute interview • Simple, natural answers • Focus on supporting students

Interview prep for Peter

Interview Strategy

  • Keep most answers around 30–60 seconds.
  • Speak slowly and clearly. You do not need complicated English.
  • Connect your teaching experience, your Education Assistant studies, and your interest in supporting students.
  • Show that you are patient, responsible, respectful, and willing to learn.
  • If you do not know something, explain that you would ask Pathways staff and follow their procedures.

1. Tell me about yourself.

Hi, my name is Peter. I have a background in education and I have teaching experience from China. Currently, I am studying in an Education Assistant program in Burnaby. I am interested in working with children and teenagers in Canada, and I would like to gain more experience supporting students in a Canadian educational environment. That is one of the reasons I am very interested in volunteering with Pathways to Education.
Tip: Aim for about 30–45 seconds.

2. Why do you want to volunteer with Pathways to Education?

I want to volunteer with Pathways because I believe education can make a big difference in a young person's life. I understand that Pathways supports high school students academically and also provides mentoring and other support. I would like to use my teaching experience to help students, and at the same time learn more about how to support diverse students in Canada.

3. What experience do you have working with young people?

I worked as a teacher in China, so I have experience communicating with students and helping them learn. I understand that students have different learning styles and different needs. I am also currently studying to become an Education Assistant, where I am learning more about inclusion, student development, behaviour support, and how to support students with different needs.

4. What subjects could you tutor?

I am especially comfortable helping students with Math. I can also support students with basic computer skills and some other school subjects. English is my second language, so for more advanced English courses I may need some preparation, but I am very comfortable helping students learn and stay organized.
Tip: Only mention subjects you truly feel comfortable supporting.

5. What would you do if a student does not understand something?

First, I would try to understand exactly what the student is having difficulty with. Then I would explain it in a simpler way or break the task into smaller steps. I might use an example, a diagram, or another way of explaining the idea. I would also give the student time to think instead of immediately giving them the answer. My goal would be to help the student understand and become more independent.

6. What would you do if a student does not want to work?

I would stay calm and try to build a connection with the student. I might ask them what is difficult or what they need help with. Sometimes a student may be frustrated or may not understand the work. I would try to encourage them and perhaps start with a smaller or easier task. If there was an ongoing problem, I would communicate with Pathways staff and follow their guidance.

7. How would you build a relationship with a student?

I think trust takes time. I would listen to the student, treat them with respect, and try to understand their interests and goals. I would also be patient and consistent. I do not think a volunteer should only focus on homework. Sometimes encouraging a student and showing that you believe in them can also be very important.

8. What would you do if a student told you something personal or concerning?

I would listen calmly and respectfully, but I would not promise to keep something secret if it involved the student's safety. I would follow Pathways' policies and report the concern to the appropriate staff member.
Key idea: Listen, stay calm, protect confidentiality appropriately, and follow staff procedures.

9. What are your strengths?

I think my strengths are patience, responsibility, and teaching experience. I am comfortable explaining things step by step, and I enjoy helping students understand something that was difficult for them. I am also willing to learn and take feedback from staff.

10. What is one area you would like to improve?

Because I am still learning about the Canadian school system, I would like to become more familiar with how Canadian schools support students with different backgrounds and learning needs. That is also one reason this volunteer opportunity is valuable to me.

Most Important Answer to Practice

Question: Why Pathways?

I have teaching experience, and I am currently studying to become an Education Assistant. My long-term goal is to work in education in Canada. I really enjoy supporting students, and I think Pathways would give me an opportunity to contribute to the community while also learning more about supporting diverse high school students.

Questions You Can Ask Atania

  • What does a typical volunteer tutoring session look like?
  • What kind of training or support do volunteers receive?
  • What subjects do you currently need the most volunteer support for?
  • Do volunteers normally work with the same students each week, or with different students?
Best choice: Ask the first two questions if there is enough time.

Likely 20-Minute Interview Flow

2–3 minIntroductions and “Tell me about yourself.”
10–12 minExperience, motivation, availability, and student-support questions.
3–5 minProgram information and your questions.

Before the Call

Final Reminder

You do not need to memorize every sentence. Remember the main ideas: teaching experience, EA studies, patience, respect, student support, willingness to learn, and following staff guidance.
