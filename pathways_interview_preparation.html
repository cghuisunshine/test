Pathways to Education

Volunteer Tutor – Phone Interview Preparation

Pacific Community Resources Society • Downtown Eastside & Strathcona • Grades 8–12

20-minute call with Atania • Simple, natural answers • Focus on supporting students

Interview prep for Peter

Interview Strategy

  • Keep most answers around 30–60 seconds.
  • Speak slowly and clearly. You do not need complicated English.
  • Everything you say should match what you wrote on your application form. Atania will likely have it in front of her.
  • Connect your engineering and BCIT background, your Education Assistant studies, and your interest in supporting students.
  • Show that you are patient, responsible, respectful, non-judgmental, and willing to learn.
  • If you do not know something, say you would ask Pathways staff and follow their procedures.

Program Facts to Know

Straight from the application form. Mentioning one or two of these shows you did your homework.

  • Pathways is a free program run by Pacific Community Resources Society (PCRS), in partnership with local schools, Ray-Cam Cooperative Centre and Strathcona Community Centre.
  • It supports youth living in the Downtown Eastside and Strathcona area to finish high school and move on to post-secondary education or meaningful employment.
  • Tutors give academic support to youth in grades 8–12, both one-to-one and in small groups.
  • Core subjects: English, Math, Science, Social Sciences, French, Spanish and other languages.
  • Commitment: one session per week, up to 3 hours, for the full school year October to June.
  • Volunteers attend an orientation and at least two ongoing training sessions.
  • Must be 19 or older, and complete a Criminal Record Check (Vulnerable Sector) before orientation. PCRS emails you a secure link.
  • They want volunteers who are friendly, welcoming and non-judgmental, and who respect and value diversity.

What You Wrote on Your Application

Stay consistent with these. Do not add a different story on the phone.

  • Bachelor’s degree in Engineering (China).
  • Full Stack Web Development at BCIT, completed with Distinction.
  • Experience through informal tutoring and educational support.
  • Comfortable with math, science, basic computer skills, and general study skills.
  • Availability preference: 1st Monday, 2nd Wednesday, 3rd Thursday.
  • You heard about Pathways through the PCRS website.
  • You said you have no other commitments that would stop you finishing the year in June.
One update to mention: you are now studying in an Education Assistant program. That started after you sent the form, so introduce it as news — “since I submitted my application, I have also started an Education Assistant program.” It is a strong point in your favour.

1. Tell me about yourself.

Hi, my name is Peter. I have a Bachelor’s degree in Engineering from China, and after I came to Canada I completed a Full Stack Web Development program at BCIT, which I finished with Distinction. I have also been helping students informally with school subjects, and since I sent my application I have started studying in an Education Assistant program. I really enjoy supporting students and helping them build confidence, so I am very interested in tutoring with Pathways.
Tip: Aim for about 30–45 seconds.

2. Why do you want to volunteer with Pathways to Education?

I enjoy supporting students and helping them build confidence in their learning, and I believe education can make a meaningful difference in a young person’s future. I also like that Pathways is a free program for youth in the Downtown Eastside and Strathcona area, and that it supports them through high school and on to post-secondary education or work. I would like to contribute my time and skills to help students feel encouraged, understood, and capable.

3. What experience do you have working with youth?

I have supported young learners through informal tutoring and educational support. I have helped students understand school subjects by breaking difficult concepts into simpler steps and encouraging them to keep trying. I am patient, responsible, and respectful, and I understand that every student learns differently. In my Education Assistant program I am also learning more about inclusion, student development, behaviour support, and how to help students with different needs.

4. What subjects could you tutor for grades 8 to 12?

I am most comfortable with Math and Science, and I can also help with basic computer skills and general study skills like organization and planning. My background in engineering and web development means I am used to breaking a complex problem into clear, step-by-step logic, which works well for math and science. English is my second language, so for senior English or Social Studies I would be more comfortable helping a student understand the assignment and plan their work rather than teaching advanced essay writing.
Tip: Pathways lists English, Math, Science, Social Sciences, French and Spanish as core subjects. Be honest about which ones you can really support — being clear is better than over-promising.

5. Can you commit to one session every week from October to June?

Yes. I understand the commitment is one session a week, up to about three hours, for the whole school year from October to June, and I am able to do that. On my application I put Monday as my first choice, Wednesday second, and Thursday third, and any of those days work for me. I do not have other commitments that would stop me from staying until the end of June, and if anything unexpected ever came up I would let staff know in advance.
Also confirm if asked: you are over 19, you are happy to complete the Criminal Record Check (Vulnerable Sector), and you will attend the orientation and the ongoing training sessions.

6. What would you do if a student does not understand something?

First, I would try to find out exactly what the student is having difficulty with. Then I would explain it in a simpler way, or break the task into smaller steps. I might use an example, a diagram, or a different way of explaining the same idea. I would also give the student time to think instead of immediately giving them the answer. My goal is for the student to understand it themselves and become more independent.

7. What would you do if a student does not want to work?

I would stay calm and try to build a connection with the student first. I might ask them what feels difficult, or what they need help with. Sometimes a student is frustrated, tired, or does not understand the work. I would encourage them and maybe start with a smaller or easier task so they get one success. If it kept happening, I would talk to Pathways staff and follow their guidance.

8. How would you build a relationship with a student?

I think trust takes time. I would listen to the student, treat them with respect, and try to understand their interests and their goals. I would also be patient and consistent, and show up every week. I do not think a tutor should only focus on homework. Sometimes encouraging a student and showing that you believe in them is just as important.

9. Pathways students come from many different backgrounds. How would you make sure every student feels welcome?

I would be friendly and non-judgmental, and treat every student with respect. I would not make assumptions about a student’s situation or about why they are struggling. I would listen, be patient, and make it clear that it is okay not to understand something yet. I also know that students in the Downtown Eastside and Strathcona area may be dealing with things outside of school, so being calm, consistent and encouraging matters just as much as the academic help.
Key words: friendly, welcoming, non-judgmental, respect diversity. These come straight from the Pathways volunteer description.

10. What would you do if a student told you something personal or concerning?

I would listen calmly and respectfully, but I would not promise to keep something secret if it involved the student’s safety. I would follow Pathways’ policies and report the concern to the appropriate staff member.
Key idea: Listen, stay calm, do not promise secrecy about safety, and follow staff procedures.

11. What are your strengths?

I would say patience, clear communication, and problem-solving. My engineering and web development background trained me to break a complex idea into clear, step-by-step explanations, and that is very useful when tutoring. I am also reliable, and I am willing to learn and take feedback from staff.

12. What is one area you would like to improve?

I am still learning about the Canadian school system, so I would like to become more familiar with the BC curriculum for grades 8 to 12, and with how Canadian schools support students from different backgrounds and with different learning needs. That is one of the reasons the orientation and ongoing training at Pathways is valuable to me.

Most Important Answer to Practice

Question: Why Pathways?

I enjoy supporting students and helping them build confidence. I have a background in engineering and web development, so I am comfortable explaining things step by step, and I am currently studying to become an Education Assistant. My long-term goal is to work in education in Canada. Pathways would let me contribute to the Downtown Eastside and Strathcona community while gaining hands-on experience supporting high school students with different needs.

Questions You Can Ask Atania

  • What does a typical tutoring session look like at Ray-Cam or Strathcona Community Centre?
  • What does the volunteer orientation and the ongoing training cover?
  • Which subjects do you need tutors for the most right now?
  • Do tutors usually work with the same students each week, or with different students?
  • When would the Criminal Record Check and the orientation take place?
Best choice: Ask the first two if there is enough time. They show you are thinking about actually doing the role well.
