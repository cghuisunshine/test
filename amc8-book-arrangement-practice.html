Question 01 / 03Intermediate

French and Italian books

A student has 8 different books on a shelf: 3 French, 2 Italian, and 3 Japanese. How many arrangements are possible if all 3 French books must be together and both Italian books must be together?

F1F2F3I1I2J1J2J3

Understand the solution
  1. Make one French block (FFF) and one Italian block (II).
  2. These two blocks and the 3 different Japanese books give 5 objects, which can be arranged in 5! = 120 ways.
  3. Within the French block, there are 3! = 6 orders. Within the Italian block, there are 2! = 2 orders.
  4. Multiply these independent choices.
5! × 3! × 2! = 1,440

Correct choice: B

Question 02 / 03Intermediate

Science books and novels

A teacher has 10 different books: 2 science books, 3 history books, and 5 novels. How many arrangements are possible if the science books must be next to each other and all 5 novels must also be next to each other?

S1S2H1H2H3N1N2N3N4N5

Understand the solution
  1. Group the science books into (SS) and the novels into (NNNNN).
  2. The 2 groups plus the 3 different history books form 5 objects: arrange them in 5! = 120 ways.
  3. Arrange the science books within their block in 2! ways, and the novels within their block in 5! ways.
  4. Multiply the counts.
5! × 2! × 5! = 28,800

Correct choice: C

Question 03 / 03Challenge

Three subject groups

A professor has 10 different textbooks: 3 mathematics, 3 physics, and 4 chemistry. How many ways can the books be arranged if all books of the same subject must stay together?

M1M2M3P1P2P3C1C2C3C4

Understand the solution
  1. Make three blocks: (MMM), (PPP), and (CCCC).
  2. Arrange these 3 blocks in 3! = 6 ways.
  3. Inside the blocks, independently arrange the mathematics books in 3! ways, the physics books in 3! ways, and the chemistry books in 4! ways.
  4. Multiply all four factors.
3! × 3! × 3! × 4! = 5,184

Correct choice: D
